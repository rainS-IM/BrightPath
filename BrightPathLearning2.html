Science

Science explores the methods and experiments that describe how the natural world behaves. Use the quick links below to jump into focused notes, experiments, and practice problems.

Core Focus Areas

  • Physics: Motion, forces, energy, and practical problem solving.
  • Chemistry: Reactions, the periodic table, and lab-safe experiments.
  • Biology: Cells, ecosystems, and interpreting biological data.

Physics Essentials

Newton's Laws of Motion: Objects at rest stay at rest unless acted upon by force. Force equals mass times acceleration.

Types of Energy: Kinetic energy (motion), potential energy (stored), thermal energy (heat), and more.

Key Concepts: Velocity, acceleration, friction, gravity, momentum, and work.

Chemistry Guide

The Periodic Table: Elements organized by atomic number. Each element has unique properties.

Chemical Reactions: Reactants combine to form products. Reactions can be exothermic or endothermic.

Bonding: Ionic bonds (electron transfer), covalent bonds (electron sharing), and hydrogen bonds.

Biology Fundamentals

Cell Structure: Nucleus controls the cell, mitochondria produces energy, ribosomes make proteins.

DNA & Genetics: DNA carries genetic information. Genes are inherited from parents.

Evolution & Adaptation: Species evolve over time through natural selection to better suit their environment.

Geography

Geography connects physical landscapes with human systems — maps, climate, and how societies shape their environments. Use the shortcuts to find maps, diagrams, and topical summaries.

Main Tracks

Physical Terrain: Plate tectonics, climate, water cycles, and map reading.

Human Systems: Population, resources, trade networks, and urban development.

Literature

Literature helps you read with intent: identify themes, analyze structure, and practice writing clear responses. Use sample essays and structure templates to build your notes.

Core Targets

Learn to spot theme, tone, narrative voice, and how to structure an effective essay or paragraph.

Computer Science

Computer Science covers the core ideas behind programming, data, and systems. Use the resource library to store cheat-sheets, sample code, and one-line commands.

System Mechanics

  • Data Structures: Arrays, lists, stacks, and when to use each.
  • Algorithms: Sorting, searching, and basic complexity ideas.
  • Networks & Web: HTTP basics, REST, and how the internet routes data.

History

History looks at events, causes, and effects. Use timelines, primary source excerpts, and short summaries to build quick revision notes.

Economics

Economics studies production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services. It helps explain how markets and money work.

Core Concepts

  • Supply & Demand: Price is determined by how much people want and how much is available.
  • Scarcity: Resources are limited, so choices must be made about allocation.
  • Opportunity Cost: Choosing one option means giving up another.
  • Money & Banking: Currency facilitates trade. Banks manage savings and loans.

Microeconomics

Individual Behavior: How consumers and businesses make decisions about buying and selling.

Market Structure: Perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly, and monopolistic competition.

Pricing: How firms set prices based on costs, competition, and demand.

Macroeconomics

Economic Growth: GDP measures total production. Growth indicates a healthy economy.

Inflation: Rising prices reduce the value of money over time.

Employment & Unemployment: Job creation and loss affect overall economic health.

Psychology

Psychology is the study of behavior and the mind. It explores why people think, feel, and act the way they do.

Main Branches

  • Cognitive Psychology: How we think, remember, and process information.
  • Social Psychology: How people interact, influence each other, and form groups.
  • Developmental Psychology: How humans grow and change from birth through old age.
  • Clinical Psychology: Diagnosis and treatment of mental health disorders.

Learning Theories

Classical Conditioning: Stimulus-response learning. Pavlov's dogs learned to salivate at a bell.

Operant Conditioning: Behavior is shaped by rewards and punishments.

Social Learning: People learn by observing others and imitating behavior.

Emotions & Behavior

Basic Emotions: Joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, and disgust are universal.

Motivation: Drives like hunger, thirst, and the need for achievement push behavior.

Stress & Coping: How people handle challenges and manage anxiety.

Environmental Science

Environmental Science examines the natural world and human impact on ecosystems, climate, and resources.

Key Topics

  • Ecosystems: Biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components interact in food webs.
  • Climate & Weather: Atmospheric patterns, greenhouse gases, and climate change.
  • Conservation: Protecting biodiversity and sustainable resource use.
  • Pollution: Air, water, and soil pollution from human activities.

Ecosystems

Food Chains & Webs: Energy flows from producers (plants) to consumers to decomposers.

Biodiversity: The variety of species in an ecosystem. Greater diversity means greater resilience.

Biomes: Tropical rainforests, deserts, tundra, and aquatic environments have distinct characteristics.

Climate & Change

Greenhouse Effect: CO₂ and methane trap heat in the atmosphere, warming the planet.

Global Warming: Rising temperatures cause melting ice, rising sea levels, and extreme weather.

Carbon Cycle: Carbon moves between atmosphere, oceans, soil, and living organisms.

Sustainability

Renewable Resources: Solar, wind, hydro power that can be replenished.

Conservation: Protecting forests, wetlands, and endangered species.

Green Living: Reduce waste, recycle, use sustainable products, and support eco-friendly practices.
